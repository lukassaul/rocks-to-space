# Hazard Research Catalog

*Initial topic inventory — November 2026*

This catalog is a **queue of questions to investigate**, not a list of predicted catastrophes. Entries are **not yet assessed**. Inclusion means a topic may warrant source review or a discriminating test; it does not indicate endorsement, urgency, or likelihood.

Use the [Hazard Assessment Template](../../../Templates/Hazard_Assessment_Template.md) for each new entry. See the [parent overview](../README.md) for the distinction between human disruption, mass extinction, and total biosphere sterilization.

## Candidate topics

| Topic | Area | Starting question | Initial handling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Large asteroid impacts | Asteroids and comets | What objects have quantified impact probabilities, and what would the consequences be? | Established physical hazard; use live monitoring |
| Long-period comet impacts | Asteroids and comets | What are the detection limits, encounter rates, and possible consequences? | Quantitative literature review |
| Extreme solar storms | Solar and stellar | What is the evidence for severe space-weather effects on infrastructure and ecosystems? | Established mechanism; separate types of harm |
| Solar superflares | Solar and stellar | What do observations of solar-type stars imply, and what remains uncertain for our Sun? | Evidence and model review |
| Long-term solar brightening | Solar and stellar | How does solar evolution limit Earth's habitability? | Established process; see existing habitability chapter |
| Nearby supernovae | Solar and stellar | Which distances and conditions could affect Earth's atmosphere or biosphere? | Established phenomenon; assess exposure |
| Gamma-ray bursts | Solar and stellar | What are plausible exposure conditions and biological effects? | Established phenomenon; assess rare-event models |
| Galactic cosmic-ray environment | Galactic | What changes in exposure are supported by measurements and models? | Investigate mechanism and uncertainty |
| Galactic magnetic/current-sheet crossings | Galactic | Are proposed catastrophic Earth effects supported by observations and causal models? | **Speculative catastrophe claim**; seek discriminating tests |
| Proposed recurrent solar “mini-nova” catastrophes | Solar and stellar | Is there evidence that the Sun undergoes such events, distinct from ordinary solar activity and stellar novae? | **Unsupported as an established solar catastrophe forecast**; test specific claims |
| Large igneous provinces and supereruptions | Terrestrial | What mechanisms and consequences are supported by geological records? | Evidence and recurrence review |
| Rapid ecological collapse | Terrestrial | Which ecosystem feedbacks and tipping processes are evidenced? | Active scientific research; avoid global overgeneralization |
| Severe pandemics | Biological and societal | Which scenarios threaten societies, species, or ecological systems? | Mechanism-specific review |
| Other proposed hazards | Open submissions | What is the exact claim, strongest evidence, counterevidence, and discriminating test? | Triage without automatic endorsement |

These labels are **starting descriptions**, not completed ratings. A formal entry must cite sources before assigning its evidence, severity, timing, testability, actionability, and research-priority assessments.

## Triage workflow

1. **Submit the claim**, with its source and a precise mechanism. No claims of a confirmed threat without evidence.
2. **Check for duplication** and distinguish it from known physical phenomena with similar names.
3. **Collect supporting and opposing sources**, preferably primary research.
4. **Complete the six independent assessment dimensions** using the template.
5. **Identify a testable question** and at least one plausible near-term scientific or societal benefit.
6. **Invite domain experts to critique the entry**; document disagreements and corrections.
7. **Review, revise, or archive** the entry as evidence changes.

There is no requirement to give every proposed scenario equal time or funding. We welcome leads broadly while allocating effort according to evidence, testability, expected learning, and opportunity cost. Unsupported claims can be documented concisely and closed or archived; openness does not require endless pursuit.

## Outreach potential

Each entry may serve as a collaboration brief: what is known, what is contested, what observations would help, and what expertise is needed. Suitable outreach might connect planetary-defense specialists, astronomers, solar physicists, geophysicists, ecologists, statisticians, historians of science, and science communicators.

Public-facing communication must distinguish **investigating a possibility** from **warning of a demonstrated danger**. Avoid sensational dates, unsupported probabilities, or claims of expert endorsement.
