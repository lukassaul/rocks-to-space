# Existential Risks and the Survival of Life

*Research overview — November 2026*

## Purpose

Life is precious, and its survival is not guaranteed. This chapter surveys hazards to Earth's biosphere and asks which outcomes they could plausibly cause, when, with what uncertainty, and what research or prevention could help. **Urgency about protecting life does not require exaggerating the probability of any particular catastrophe.**

The scientific assessment of hazards belongs here; the moral reasons to respond and constraints on intervention are developed in [Life, Gaia, and Humanity's Role](../Life_Gaia_and_Humanity/README.md) and [Ethics and Planetary Protection](../Ethics_and_Planetary_Protection/README.md).

## Distinguish the outcomes

Do not use *existential*, *extinction*, or *sterilization* interchangeably. Assess separately:

1. Severe regional or global disruption to human societies.
2. Mass extinction or major ecological reorganization.
3. Human extinction.
4. Loss of complex surface ecosystems.
5. Loss of surface habitability.
6. Extinction of all terrestrial life, including sheltered microbial refuges.

A hazard may reach one threshold without reaching the others. The relevant mechanisms, refuges, recovery pathways, and confidence levels must be specified.

## Hazard families

| Family | Questions to investigate | Evidence cautions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present-day terrestrial pressures | Climate change, habitat loss, biodiversity decline, pollution, ecological resilience | Documented current harms do not imply imminent total planetary sterilization. |
| Geological and biological hazards | Large volcanic episodes, disease, cascading ecosystem disruption | Quantify severity, geographic scope, and biological recovery. |
| Asteroids and comets | Impact energies, encounter probabilities, warning time, mitigation | Use continuously updated orbital risk assessments; close approaches are not automatically predicted impacts. |
| Solar hazards | Solar storms, flares, extreme space weather, long-term luminosity change | Separate technological disruption from biological extinction and eventual loss of habitability. |
| Stellar hazards | Nearby supernovae and gamma-ray bursts | Effects depend strongly on distance, energy, geometry, and atmospheric response; avoid unsupported event dates. |
| Galactic environment | Cosmic-ray exposure, magnetic structures, Solar System environment | Distinguish observed structures from proposed causal links to terrestrial catastrophes. |
| Speculative catastrophic scenarios | Proposed solar “mini-nova” cycles or catastrophic galactic current-sheet crossings | Do not present these as established physical predictions; identify testable claims and contrary evidence. |
| Human-caused risks | Environmental destruction and other technological or systemic hazards | Assess specific mechanisms rather than treating all risks as equivalent. |

Long-term solar evolution and the finite duration of Earth's habitability are covered in depth in [Earth's Finite Habitability and the Survival of Life](../Long_Term_Habitability_of_Earth/README.md).

## A living hazard register, not a prophecy calendar

For each proposed threat, record:

- **Mechanism:** what physical or biological process is claimed?
- **Evidence status:** established observation, supported model, open hypothesis, or unsupported assertion.
- **Possible consequences:** which of the six thresholds could it reach?
- **Time horizon and likelihood:** with uncertainty and a source date; state when not quantifiable.
- **Early warning and mitigation:** what can be measured or done now?
- **Scientific sources:** primary literature and current monitoring authorities.
- **Review date:** when the assessment should be revisited.

In particular, do not maintain a static list of supposed asteroid “danger dates” without live orbital solutions and explicit impact probabilities. Consult [NASA CNEOS Sentry](https://cneos.jpl.nasa.gov/sentry/) and the [ESA NEO Coordination Centre](https://neo.ssa.esa.int/) for monitored impact risks.

## Research questions

- Which hazards threaten humanity, complex ecosystems, or all life, and how do their thresholds differ?
- How resilient are microbial refuges to extreme conditions?
- Which low-probability hazards justify present-day preparedness?
- What uncertainties can observations, simulations, or experiments reduce?
- How can hazard research benefit biodiversity protection, resilience, and planetary defense today?

## Scientific practice

Make room for unconventional hypotheses, but do not give all hypotheses equal evidential weight. Distinguish observation, mechanism, inference, and speculation; seek falsifiable predictions; document negative results; update assessments when evidence changes.
