## 4. Independent assessment dimensions

These dimensions are **not a single numerical risk score**. Do not convert a spectacular claimed outcome into a high-probability risk without evidence.

| Dimension | Assessment | Basis and uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Evidence strength** | Established / Supported hypothesis / Speculative / Unsupported | |
| **Potential severity** | Localized / Civilizational / Mass extinction / Potential biosphere sterilization / Unknown | Specify *conditional* consequences; distinguish claims from supported models. |
| **Likelihood and timing** | Quantified / Constrained / Highly uncertain / Unknown | Include probability and time horizon only when defensible. |
| **Scientific testability** | Strong / Moderate / Weak / Not yet testable | Name discriminating observations. |
| **Actionability** | Existing interventions / Research opportunities / Monitoring only / No identified intervention | |
| **Research priority** | High / Medium / Low / Deferred | Explain tractability, scientific value, near-term benefits, and opportunity cost. |

**Evidence strength definitions**
- **Established:** mechanism or hazard is well supported by observations and physical understanding; this does *not* establish a particular catastrophic prediction.
- **Supported hypothesis:** credible evidence and a testable mechanism exist, with important unresolved questions.
- **Speculative:** mechanism or causal connection remains conjectural, with inadequate confirming evidence.
- **Unsupported:** the specific claim lacks adequate evidence, contradicts robust findings, or lacks a viable mechanism; document the reasons rather than dismissing by label alone.

**Severity:** Always specify which outcome is being discussed: societal disruption, mass extinction, human extinction, loss of complex life, loss of surface habitability, or loss of *all* life including microbial refuges. Severity is conditional on the scenario actually occurring.

**Likelihood:** Unknown is not equivalent to zero, and it is not evidence of a high probability. Avoid invented dates, recurrence intervals, and impact probabilities.

**Research priority:** High priority means a promising investigation, **not** a high probability of catastrophe. Favor discriminating tests, credible dual benefits, and tractable collaboration.
