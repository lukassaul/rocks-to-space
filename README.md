# Rocks2Space

**Research toward directed panspermia and the flowering of life beyond Earth.**

## Vision

Rocks2Space is a long-term research initiative investigating scientific, technological, and organizational pathways toward spreading carbon-based life beyond Earth, potentially throughout the Milky Way. We call this overarching aspiration **flowering**.

The ultimate objective is extraordinarily distant in time. Our immediate objective is to organize rigorous research that helps evaluate the feasibility, consequences, and ethics of directed panspermia while improving life on Earth.

## Founding philosophy: the responsibility of capability

**With the power to influence life's future comes the responsibility to consider it.** Rocks2Space adopts this as an explicit philosophical commitment, not as a scientific conclusion or a duty that all researchers must share.

Panspermia raises the possibility that life may have traveled between worlds before reaching Earth. That history is unproven. Our *flowering* metaphor asks whether, at a stage when humanity can study such journeys deliberately, we should investigate how life might one day **go to seed** beyond Earth. The commitment is to responsible inquiry—not an automatic obligation or authorization to spread organisms.

Protecting Earth's living biosphere comes first. Any future dissemination would require evidence, planetary protection, ethical scrutiny, and legitimate governance. See [Vision and Motivation](Strategy/Vision_and_Motivation/README.md) and [Ethics and Planetary Protection](Foundations/Ethics_and_Planetary_Protection/README.md).

## Guiding principle: benefits along the way

Every stage of the research program should be evaluated for credible near-term benefits to society, including scientific discovery, useful technologies, commercial opportunities, new businesses, employment, education, and economic development.

**The long-term vision provides direction; near-term benefits provide independent value and justification.**

Potential spin-offs are hypotheses to investigate, not guaranteed outcomes. Research proposals should specify plausible applications, beneficiaries, evidence, and measurable milestones.

## Organization

**Payload** and **Delivery** are the two primary technical research branches:

- **Payload:** Biological systems and supporting materials that might enable the establishment of self-sustaining carbon-based life.
- **Delivery:** Technologies and strategies for transporting, protecting, and depositing payloads at suitable destinations.

Four cross-cutting areas support these branches:

- **[Foundations](Foundations/README.md):** Panspermia literature, history, origin-of-life research, astrobiology, existential risks, Gaia and humanity's role, and ethics and planetary protection.
- **Strategy:** Vision, roadmaps, funding, collaboration, and governance.
- **Impact:** Societal benefits, technological spin-offs, commercialization, and workforce development.
- **Templates:** Reusable literature-review, research-proposal, and grant-proposal formats.

## Research workflow

Each level of the research tree can collect papers, develop syntheses, and support proposals. The intended workflow is to:

1. Collect and cite primary literature, preserving source provenance.
2. Distinguish demonstrated findings, plausible engineering, and speculation.
3. Identify uncertainties, competing hypotheses, and tractable research questions.
4. Develop reviews, models, experiments, and fundable proposals.
5. Evaluate immediate and intermediate societal and economic benefits.
6. Revisit priorities as evidence and ethical understanding evolve.

Store each source in its primary intellectual home and reference it from other branches rather than duplicating it.

## Stewardship across time

**We are responsible to life across time: its deep past, its living present, and its distant future. We seek to consider all three, prioritizing the protection of life today while preserving its possibilities for tomorrow.**

See the [Ethics and Planetary Protection framework](Foundations/Ethics_and_Planetary_Protection/README.md).

## Scientific responsibility

This repository is a research initiative, **not** a claim that directed panspermia is feasible or ethically justified today. Any potential deployment must be evaluated against planetary-protection requirements, the possibility of indigenous extraterrestrial life, ecological uncertainty, and appropriate international governance. Research should generate value even if deployment is never pursued.

## Long-term perspective

The flowering of life across the galaxy, if achievable and justified, may take many generations. The research can create value long before that distant outcome.

**We pursue the far future by creating value in the present.**
