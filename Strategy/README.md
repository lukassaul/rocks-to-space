# Strategy: Building an International Research Community Around Rocks2Space

*Initial strategic overview — November 2026*

## 1. An invitation to researchers

Rocks2Space is a long-term interdisciplinary initiative investigating the survival, transport, and potential dissemination of life beyond Earth. Its questions connect astrobiology, **geology and lithopanspermia**, microbiology, genomics, evolutionary biology, physics, spacecraft engineering, computer science, ecology, and social science.

The eventual deliberate dissemination of life is speculative and subject to profound scientific, ecological, and governance uncertainties. Participation does **not** require endorsing that goal. Researchers can contribute valuable critical tests, methods, and discoveries within their own fields.

**The initiative's purpose is to help researchers advance their own fields by connecting them to complementary expertise, productive questions, shared resources, new funding mechanisms, and an engaging long-term scientific challenge.** Its [Impact overview](../Impact/README.md) explains the dual-benefit principle: research should produce independently valuable near-term outcomes even if the distant vision never materializes.

## 2. What researchers could gain

Rocks2Space aims to provide new interdisciplinary questions; potential collaborators; shared literature, data, and simulations; routes to grants and philanthropic support; technical challenges and prizes; conferences and workshops; student participation; and public engagement opportunities. These are **capabilities to develop**, not services already established.

The initial model should be a distributed network of independent laboratories, universities, and individual researchers, rather than a centrally managed laboratory.

## 3. Where different disciplines fit

### Geology, mineralogy, geochemistry, and impact science

The **rocks** in Rocks2Space are fundamental. Lithopanspermia concerns biological material transported within impact-ejected rock. Geological questions include impact fragmentation and ejecta velocities; shock metamorphism; pressure-temperature histories; porosity and fractures; mineral shielding against radiation; weathering; meteorite preservation; and microbial habitats within rocks.

Geologists, mineralogists, geochemists, and impact physicists could contribute laboratory characterization, meteorite studies, impact simulations, field research, and experiments on the physical protection afforded by different mineral matrices. These studies can improve impact science, materials characterization, and environmental microbiology independently of interstellar applications.

### Planetary science and celestial mechanics

Study ejecta dynamics, escape trajectories, gravity assists, interstellar transport, stellar encounters, and the distinction between flythrough and permanent capture. The [Delivery overview](../Delivery/README.md) and [Jupiter flyby research questions](../Delivery/Trajectory_Design/Jupiter_Flyby_and_Stellar_Capture/README.md) provide starting points. Useful outcomes include validated trajectory tools and uncertainty-propagation methods.

### Microbiology and biological preservation

Investigate survival under radiation, vacuum, desiccation, thermal extremes, and dormancy, using controlled experiments and appropriate biosafety. Compare biological protection within mineral matrices. Produce preservation protocols, survival datasets, and mechanistic insights.

### Genetics, genomics, and bioinformatics

Sequence and compare extremophile genomes; investigate DNA repair and stress-resistance pathways; develop predictive bioinformatics and experimentally testable hypotheses. Engineered organisms can serve as controlled laboratory models, but any proposed extraterrestrial release would require separate, exceptionally rigorous biosafety and planetary-protection assessment.

### Evolutionary biology and ecology

Investigate adaptation, genetic stability, ecosystem establishment, and evolutionary transitions. Distinguish survival from successful establishment, and do not assume complex life is an inevitable evolutionary outcome.

### Aerospace engineering and autonomy

Study precision propulsion, small spacecraft, Jupiter-flyby navigation, trajectory corrections, autonomous fault recovery, and long-duration reliability. Benchmark practical benefits for present-day spacecraft and robotics.

### Mathematics, statistics, and computer science

Develop open numerical models, optimization tools, rare-event analysis, uncertainty quantification, and reproducible simulations of biological survival and material transport.

### Education, social science, philosophy, and ethics

Study science education, cooperation, psychological responses to long-term shared purpose, stewardship of Gaia, and governance of potential biological dissemination. Evaluate social benefits rather than assuming them. Encourage critical perspectives and inclusive participation.

## 4. A distributed collaboration model

Existing institutions already provide laboratories, equipment, expertise, and research administration. Rocks2Space should connect rather than duplicate them.

For example, a geological group might characterize shock-damaged rocks; microbiologists could measure survival within comparable materials; bioinformaticians could study stress-response pathways; and dynamicists could model transport conditions. Shared protocols, benchmark datasets, joint papers, and grant proposals could make these efforts mutually informative.

Researchers should be able to participate through independent studies, public datasets, software, critical reviews, student projects, or collaborations without signing on to the entire long-term vision.

## 5. Funding through established institutions

Funding should usually be sought for **bounded projects that fit an institution's existing mandate**, not initially for a single grand mission. Potential ecosystems to investigate include:

| Funding ecosystem | Potential fit |

| US National Science Foundation | Geology, biology, engineering, computing, education |
| European Research Council | Investigator-led frontier science |
| European Space Agency | Space science, technology, and mission concepts |
| UK Research and Innovation | Geoscience, bioscience, engineering, astronomy |

These are **institutional leads, not verified current calls or confirmed sponsors**. Eligibility, national restrictions, grant amounts, deadlines, and institutional fit must be checked for each proposal. Research partners and grant-making bodies must be distinguished.

## 6. Independent research foundations and philanthropy

Independent foundations may support exploratory research, interdisciplinary work, education, and scientific infrastructure. Organizations worth investigating by name include the **John Templeton Foundation**, **Simons Foundation**, **Alfred P. Sloan Foundation**, **Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation**, **Heising-Simons Foundation**, **Schmidt Sciences**, and **Breakthrough Initiatives**.

These names indicate possible mission overlap, **not an endorsement, open funding opportunity, or willingness to fund Rocks2Space**. Proposals should match each funder's actual priorities. A genomics study, interstellar-navigation simulation, and education pilot require different cases for support.

Donor-funded work should have transparent budgets, independent scientific review, and safeguards against donor influence over research conclusions.

## 7. An independent Rocks2Space organization

A future nonprofit or research foundation might coordinate donations, administer small grants and fellowships, support conferences, maintain shared software, sponsor prizes, and facilitate partnerships.

However, incorporation entails legal, accounting, compliance, staffing, and governance costs. **Fiscal sponsorship, university hosting, or partnerships with existing nonprofits** may be more practical at first. An independent organization should emerge only when sustained participation and funding justify it.

Possible research-fund mechanisms, subject to resources and governance:

| Mechanism | Illustrative award range |
| --- | ---: |
| Student microgrants | $1,000–$5,000 |
| Open-source simulation awards | $5,000–$20,000 |
| Exploratory interdisciplinary studies | $10,000–$50,000 |
| Technical challenge prizes | $5,000–$100,000 |
| Collaborative pilot grants | $25,000–$150,000 |

These figures are planning illustrations, not commitments or verified costs.

## 8. Research prizes, rewards, and competitions

Challenge prizes can attract researchers, students, engineers, and independent programmers to well-defined technical problems. Candidate competitions include:

- **Precision Navigation Challenge:** minimize modeled uncertainty in Jupiter-assisted outbound trajectories.
- **Stellar Capture Challenge:** identify and validate trajectories with clearly specified capture criteria, distinguishing temporary binding from lasting capture.
- **Biological Resilience Modeling Challenge:** predict measured survival outcomes from published or appropriately governed datasets.
- **Extremophile Bioinformatics Challenge:** develop reproducible tools to identify stress-resistance mechanisms.
- **Educational Simulation Challenge:** build accurate, accessible tools for teaching lithopanspermia, orbital dynamics, or planetary protection.

Competitions require published evaluation criteria, independent judging, reproducible benchmarks, fair participation rules, appropriate IP and licensing terms, and attention to biosafety and research ethics. Prizes should reward verifiable advances, not untestable claims.

## 9. Scientific conferences, workshops, and community building

**Regular scientific meetings should be a core strategy for both building research teams and improving the quality of the science.** Many specialists working on related problems may not otherwise encounter each other's methods or results.

### Annual international Rocks2Space conference

An annual scientific conference could bring together geology, impact science, astrobiology, microbiology, genomics, evolutionary biology, celestial mechanics, engineering, ethics, and education. Sessions might cover lithopanspermia and mineral shielding; biological survival; interstellar dynamics and stellar capture; spacecraft technology; ecosystem modeling; planetary protection; and social impact.

The conference should welcome skeptical and critical work as well as supportive research. It must not become an echo chamber.

### Scientific quality and critical review

Scientific programs could use reviewed abstracts, invited methodological critiques, reproducibility sessions, comparison of competing models, and presentations of negative results. A recurring **State of the Science** session could assess which hypotheses have gained evidence, remain untested, or have been weakened or rejected.

**Credibility should develop through rigorous criticism, independent verification, and openness to disconfirming evidence—not agreement with the project's distant vision.**

### Focused technical workshops

Between conferences, smaller meetings could standardize microbial survival measurements, compare impact-ejecta models, benchmark Jupiter-flyby precision, curate shared extremophile genomic data, or design reproducible technical competitions.

Each workshop should aim for concrete outputs: protocols, benchmark datasets, open-source tools, research roadmaps, or collaborative grant concepts.

### Building interdisciplinary teams

Structured collaboration sessions could connect geologists with microbiologists, dynamicists with engineers, and bioinformaticians with evolutionary biologists. Early-career researchers, independent researchers, and international institutions should have meaningful ways to participate.

### Accessible formats and conference outputs

Start with inexpensive **quarterly virtual research seminars** and focused online workshops. As participation grows, consider an **annual hybrid conference**, potentially rotating among host institutions in different countries. Accessibility measures might include low fees, remote participation, recordings, and travel support when funded.

Open abstracts, workshop summaries, and research materials can make meetings useful beyond attendees. Journal publications or special issues should follow independent editorial and peer-review standards.

### Funding and measuring conference value

Universities, scientific societies, research institutes, donors, and industry sponsors may be potential hosts or supporters; none should be presumed committed. Budgets must include coordination, accessible technology, speakers, participant support, and administration.

Measure success through **new collaborations, joint proposals, publications, reproducible methods, shared datasets, corrected scientific assumptions, and student participation**—not attendance alone.

## 10. Costs and financial realism

Costs vary radically across research types. Literature synthesis and simulations can be comparatively inexpensive using existing infrastructure; laboratory programs need personnel, equipment, materials, safety measures, and overhead; flight experiments add qualification, launch integration, operations, and regulatory costs.

Separate **network operating costs** from **individual research-project budgets**. Avoid permanent expenses before dependable funding exists. Every grant proposal should state assumptions, deliverables, contingencies, personnel requirements, and applicable institutional overhead.

The goal is a portfolio of independently worthwhile projects, not one enormous upfront funding request.

## 11. Phased development

### Phase I — Open research community

Maintain research overviews, invite critical contributions, identify prospective collaborators, develop open simulations and educational resources, and host **quarterly virtual seminars** plus an initial interdisciplinary workshop.

### Phase II — Collaborative pilots and first conference

Submit discipline-specific grant proposals, begin collaborative pilot studies, test educational outreach and technical challenges, and convene a **first annual Rocks2Space scientific conference** with a reviewed call for abstracts and collaboration sessions.

### Phase III — Sustained coordination

If participation and resources justify it, establish fiscal sponsorship or an independent organization, coordinate peer-reviewed small grants and recurring prizes, and develop an **international conference series** with rotating hosts, focused workshops, and documented scientific outcomes.

### Phase IV — Larger demonstrations where justified

Use validated findings to evaluate advanced laboratory studies, propulsion prototypes, navigation experiments, and potentially nonbiological flight demonstrations. Any biological dissemination proposal requires independent ethical, ecological, and governance assessment; it is not an automatic progression.

## 12. International participation and governance

A project concerned with life's long-term future should seek broad international collaboration and avoid capture by one country, institution, donor, or discipline. Respect applicable research laws, export controls, data governance, IP rights, and biosafety requirements.

The initiative should protect scientific independence, disclose conflicts of interest, recognize contributions fairly, and invite informed objections. Planetary protection and the possibility of indigenous extraterrestrial life must be taken seriously.

## 13. How researchers can get involved

Researchers can start by connecting existing work to a Rocks2Space question, proposing a falsifiable hypothesis, contributing a dataset or method, identifying complementary collaborators, designing a bounded fundable pilot, presenting at a seminar, entering a technical challenge, or developing educational materials.

Participation should advance the researcher's own scientific goals even if the distant panspermia objective remains unresolved or proves infeasible.

## 14. Measuring strategic success

Track interdisciplinary collaborations, publications, validated results, open datasets and software reuse, grant funding secured, independently judged prize outcomes, educational learning, student participation, and the scientific outputs of conferences.

Do not treat money raised, membership numbers, or meeting attendance as sufficient evidence of scientific progress. Ask whether Rocks2Space enabled valuable research and cooperation that otherwise would have been less likely.

## 15. Conclusion

Rocks2Space can begin as a research community rather than a costly centralized institution. Geologists can study the rocks that might transport life; microbiologists can study survival; geneticists can investigate resilience; dynamicists can model trajectories; engineers can develop navigation and autonomy; and educators and social scientists can explore the broader benefits and responsibilities.

Existing grants, independent foundations, philanthropy, prizes, technical competitions, and **regular scientific conferences** offer complementary ways to build the community and improve its work.

**Rocks2Space should succeed first by helping researchers do valuable science in their own fields—and by making that science part of a larger, rigorous, international collaboration on the future of life.**
